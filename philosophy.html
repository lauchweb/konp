Philosophy

Understanding comes before deciding.

Nothing exists on its own. A product sits within a use, a service within a situation, an experience within a set of expectations. What is visible is only part of what is happening. The rest is found in the circumstances, decisions, habits and details around it.

That is why the first question is not always the right one. What looks like a problem may have its cause elsewhere. What appears unnecessary may have a reason that is not immediately visible. What seems like a small detail can alter the way everything around it is experienced.

Simplicity is knowing what is necessary and leaving the rest alone. Detail earns its place when it changes something. Without that, it is simply another thing to account for.

A product, a service and the experience around them affect one another. A change in one can alter another, sometimes without being noticed at first. Understanding these relationships helps determine where something needs attention, and where it does not.

There is no need to make something appear to be more than it is. What is clear can remain clear. What is unnecessary can go. What is unresolved can be questioned. The aim is to understand what is already there and make only the decisions that need to be made.

Sometimes a conversation is simply a way of finding the edges of something. Once those become visible, the thing itself can be seen differently.
